# The world

What the game's universe is, as far as we can state it. Two kinds of source: the **film** the game is set in (public, summarised in our
own words) and what the **game data and play** show. Anything else is a question, listed at the end. Do not paste text from the film,
the manual or the game's strings; describe.

## The big idea

*Treasure Planet* is *Treasure Island* moved into space. Ships are wooden sailing ships that fly through a breathable medium
called the etherium, pushed by solar sails with engines to help; crews climb rigging, run cannons and board each other. The
tone is adventure: bright, painterly, a little melancholy. The film's art direction set the proportions the engine tries to keep
(about 70% 18th-century, 30% sci-fi) and a painterly light (`../remaster-style.md`).

## What the game shows

- **Fleets:** a Navy with a ship-naming prefix (`RLS`), pirates, the Procyon ironclads, and civilian trade ships (`factions.md`).
- **Terrain of a battle:** floating islands (some with gun towers, some volcanic or inhabited), asteroids and fields of them, nebulae
  that drain sail energy, storms that cross a map, mines, black holes, space whales and dragons that attack ships
  (`docs/re/`: `nebula-landmark.md`, `asteroids.md`, `volcanoes.md`, `dragons.md`).
- **How ships move:** sails collect energy (their health matters to speed), engines supply thrust, riggers repair sails, engineers
  repair hulls. Ships dock at tenders and bases to repair and resupply (`refit-resupply.md`, `docking-and-orders.md`).
- **How ships fight:** broadsides from banks of guns with arcs, boarding with a crew's fighters, torpedoes, mines, lobbed shells,
  cloaking on the submersible (`gunnery.md`, `boarding.md`, `cloaking.md`).
- **Campaign:** 13 missions led by named ships (the first has the player in a torpedo boat, `RLS Comet`, with a Navy exercise,
  a nebula called Clarke, whales and a pirate raid), see `docs/re/campaign.md`.
- **Scale:** a battle is a few ships to a few dozen; skirmish ships are 11 to 47 units long.

## Why it matters for design

- Everything that flies is a **vessel with a crew**: it has a deck, a stern, places for people. Even the Procyon machines carry
  crews, sails and riggers.
- **Technology is salvaged and bolted on**, not sleek: the world's tech is old and weathered. New parts look added.
- **Light:** the etherium glows (red, amber, violet backdrops in the game's skies); hulls are lit warm from above and cool from
  the sky.
